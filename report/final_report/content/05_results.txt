Table 5.2 and Figure 5.1 summarise the main setting, in which the visual branch was trained on crude edits and the older generator G1 and tested on edits from the unseen generator G2. CVP attributes better than every baseline on every type, naming the edited field for 87.6% to 92.4% of tampered documents. ## 5.3 BLENDED EDITS AND WHAT EACH BRANCH CATCHES

Table 5.3 and Figure 5.2 show the problem that motivated the project. From crude to blended edits the detection AUC of the visual branch drops on every type, and at its validation threshold it flags only 13.4% to 67.1% of blended edits, against 85.8% to 100% of crude ones. The rules do not notice any difference between the two: their AUC on blended edits is within 0.002 of their AUC on crude edits, and they flag every blended edit, because the signature depends only on the values. CVP keeps this property and flags 97.6% to 100% of blended edits while flagging only 1.2% to 5.8% of genuine documents.

The combination has a cost on the other side. Complete forgeries (T4) break no rule, so CVP can only catch them through the visual branch. It flags 38.2% to 65.5% of them, and its AUC on T4 (0.755 to 0.854) is lower than that of the visual branch alone (0.788 to 0.935), because the noisy-OR mixes the visual evidence with the constraint branch's score, which cannot tell a coherent edit from a genuine document. The learned constraint branch also absorbs OCR noise well: on the receipt, the bare rules flag 10.6% of genuine documents because of misread digits, while CVP flags only 1.2%.

## 5.4 THE AMBIGUITY SPLIT AND THE HYPOTHESES

On the ambiguity split, where the broken rules fit more than one field, the rules alone are at chance or below it on every type (20.0% on the transcript at a chance level of 20.0%, and 41.3% to 50.6% on the others at 44.6% to 50.0%), while they are right 94.2% to 100% of the time on unambiguous edits. CVP beats both single-modality baselines on this split for five of the six types, reaching 87.6% to 93.2%. The exception is the shop receipt (76.6% against 86.4% for the visual branch): editing the cash or the change breaks the same single rule, and the learned class probability leans towards one of the two fields and can override the heatmap. A variant with a shared prior, which spreads the probability evenly over the two fields, gets 90.9% of these edits right, but it was slightly worse on average on the validation data and so was not chosen. Table 5.4 summarises the four hypotheses.

!table Table 5.4: The Four Hypotheses and Their Outcome
!widths 8,34,20,38
Hyp. | Statement | Outcome | Evidence
H1 | The visual branch degrades from crude to blended edits; the rules do not | Supported on all six types | Visual AUC 0.972–0.999 falls to 0.788–0.933; rule AUC changes by at most 0.002
H2 | The rules alone are at chance on the ambiguity split | Supported on all six types | 20.0–50.6% against chance levels of 20.0–50.0%; 94.2–100% on unambiguous edits
H3 | CVP beats both single modalities on the ambiguity split, by a larger margin than overall | Partly supported | Best on 5 of 6 types; margin larger than overall on 3 types, equal on 1, smaller on 1
H4 | Candidate pooling beats concatenation fusion, mainly in attribution | Supported on all six types | 6.9–33.6 points better attribution; detection AUC within 0.021
!end

Concatenation fusion has access to the same evidence as CVP, yet its attribution is much worse. A network that sees a global summary of both branches learns that a document is forged far more easily than where it was forged; pooling the heatmap inside the fields suggested by the rules is what turns visual evidence into an attribution.

## 5.5 HOW MUCH VISUAL EVIDENCE IS NEEDED, AND ROBUSTNESS

Figure 5.3 changes what the visual branch saw during training. Trained on crude edits only (zero-shot), it gets almost no blended edits right (0.0% to 6.6%), yet CVP still attributes 62.1% to 92.3% of them, close to the rules alone (60.7% to 91.7%): wherever the broken rules point to a single field, the rules do the work. CVP is better than the rules alone on four types but worse on the transcript and the invoice, where the learned head trusts a heatmap that knows nothing about blended edits. With the older generator G1 (the main setting), the visual branch reaches 63.0% to 77.9% and CVP 83.0% to 95.5%, above both single modalities on every type. With the test generator itself (oracle), both exceed 97% on five types and the gap closes. CVP helps most when the visual evidence is weak but not completely missing.

CVP stays above the visual branch on five types but falls below the rules alone on the invoice and the passport, because a head fitted on clean pages keeps trusting a heatmap that resampling has made unreliable.

## 5.6 CHOOSING THE ATTRIBUTION HEAD

Six designs of the attribution head and CVP (rule) were compared on a separate validation set of blended edits from the older generator, with the rule for choosing fixed beforehand: the best mean attribution over the six types (Table 5.5). The per-class prior with heat used as probabilities won with 97.0%, against 91.1% for CVP (rule), and it is the CVP reported throughout. On the test set, the designs trade blended single-field edits against complete forgeries: the chosen design is best on blended edits (88.9% on average), the log-space designs are best on complete forgeries (up to 74.6%), and no design wins everywhere. CVP (rule) is better than the chosen head on the transcript (92.0% against 89.1%) and the ID card (88.9% against 87.6%) but much worse on the ambiguity split (76.0% against 88.1% on average), because choosing a single class throws away the true field whenever two classes fit the broken rules.

## 5.7 REAL RECEIPTS (CORD)

The first question for real documents was whether real receipts follow the rules strictly enough for this approach to be useful. On the 992 CORD receipts to which at least one rule applies, each rule holds on 96.6% to 99.7% of genuine receipts (Table 5.6, Figure 5.6), and 6.7% of genuine receipts break some rule even when every printed word is read correctly. Taking the values only at the fields CVP reads gives 7.2%, and when docTR reads those fields the figure rises to 10.9%. The extra 3.7 points are OCR errors. These false alarms are the floor for what the rules alone can achieve on real receipts.

!table Table 5.6: Receipt Rules and How Often They Hold on 1,000 Genuine CORD Receipts

total | subtotal + tax + service − discount = total (or prices already include tax) | 642 | 97.4%

With the zero-shot visual branch, which is at chance on blended edits (AUC 0.500), CVP still raises detection from 0.546 to 0.746 and attribution from 60.0% to 73.1%, and on the ambiguity split it reaches 56.2% where both single modalities are at chance. Fine-tuning the visual branch on crude edits improves CVP to 0.838 AUC and 75.6% attribution. Even the upper-bound visual branch, which has seen the forger and attributes 92.3% on its own, is improved by CVP (93.0%; 84.2% against 75.0% on blended edits). This is the order the theory predicts: the weaker the visual evidence, the more CVP adds, and it adds something in every setting.

Two limitations are visible in Figure 5.8. Real receipts cause false alarms: at the validation threshold CVP flags 11.2% to 17.3% of genuine receipts, mostly because OCR errors break a rule. And coherent forgeries are caught only as far as the visual branch sees them: 8.9% of T4 forgeries zero-shot, 13.3% after fine-tuning on crude edits and 68.9% for the upper bound.

## 5.8 REAL FORGERIES MADE BY PEOPLE (FIND IT AGAIN!)

The Find it again! forgeries were made by people with ordinary image editors. We read every scan with docTR and checked four receipt rules: the total, the change, cash covering the total, and Malaysian rounding (an adjustment of at most 0.02). Of the 987 scans available, 646 genuine and 121 forged scans had at least one rule we could check. Some rule fails on 32.2% of the forged scans against 17.2% of the genuine ones, a difference that is statistically significant (two-proportion z ≈ 3.8). The change rule alone fails on 31.4% of forged scans against 10.5% of genuine ones (Figure 5.10). Real forgers, in other words, break receipt arithmetic far more often than genuine receipts do.

!figure report/final_report/figures/findit_rules.png | 4.8 | Figure 5.10: Share of Genuine and Forged Find it again! Scans on Which Each Receipt Rule Fails

The visual branch, on the other hand, did not transfer to these forgeries (Table 5.8). Its heat separates forged from genuine test scans at chance level (AUC 0.504 to 0.527), and its hottest pixel falls inside an edited region for only 2.9% to 8.6% of forged scans. On real human forgeries, all of CVP's evidence currently comes from the rules.

Beyond the numbers, CVP differs from these detectors in what it reports. It names the edited field, lists the rules that were broken and shows why the field was chosen, which makes its decisions easy to explain and to check by hand. It also needs no paid service and runs on an ordinary laptop.
